A real-time, browser-based **isometric colony simulator** set on the surface of Mars. Players establish and sustain an off-world settlement, balancing life support thermodynamics (Oxygen, Power, Food), workforce labor, autonomous rover logistics, surface mining, and environmental hazards against a continuous, deterministic server-side simulation engine.

Play the production build at [ztm-mars-colony.vercel.app](https://ztm-mars-colony.vercel.app).

## 3D edition

The game uses Three.js with procedural models for every facility, colonists, rovers, supply capsules and meteorites. Raised Martian terrain, crater rims, rock formations, material shading and soft shadows replace the original sprite renderer. Terrain is visual only; simulation and server action rules are unchanged.

Run `npm run dev`, then open `/model-preview.html` for an isolated model showcase (no account or live colony required), or `/` to play. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan, and click buildings to inspect them. The game retains touch pinch and pan controls. A WebGL 2 capable browser is required. `npm run build` includes both pages.

## 🪐 Overview & Premise

As the Commander of Mars Expedition Base Alpha, you are responsible for the survival and expansion of a fragile pioneer outpost on the Martian regolith.

Every second corresponds to **1 simulation tick** at 1× (1,000 ticks = 1 Sol). While the page is open, simulation speed can run at 2× or 3×. Time can also advance while the page is closed, up to an 8-hour catch-up (28,800 ticks), when that setting is on. Catch-up applies batch state transitions with mathematical determinism.

A new colony starts with **50 Oxygen, 50 Power, 50 Food, 25 Ore, and 2 Electronics**, a Habitat at `(7, 7)`, a Solar Array at `(5, 7)`, an Oxygen Scrubber at `(9, 7)`, and **2 pioneer colonists**. There are no rovers, battery cells, or pending arrivals until you build and escort them in.

## ⚙️ Core Gameplay Mechanics

### Life Support Thermodynamics
* **Oxygen (O2)**: Consumed by living colonists at **3 O2/tick** each. Produced by **Oxygen Scrubbers** (+5 O2/tick). Each operational scrubber expands the colony's maximum oxygen buffer by **+5 O2** beyond the 100 base. Power and food pools are clamped at 0–100.
* **Power (PWR)**: Generated by **Solar Arrays** (+5 PWR/tick) and consumed by active facilities. At 0, it fails the life-support check the same way empty oxygen or food does.
* **Food (FOOD)**: Consumed by living colonists at **3 Food/tick** each. Produced by **Hydroponic Farms** (+5 Food/tick).
* **The Life Support Check**: If **Oxygen == 0**, **Power == 0**, or **Food == 0** at the end of a tick, every colonist takes **-2 HP**. Ticks 1–100 are a startup grace period and do not apply this damage. When all three pools are above 0, colonists recover **+1 HP/tick**, up to 100. At 0 HP the colonist dies. If none remain, the colony is `game_over`.

### Workforce & Colonist Labor
* **Labor Capacity**: Each living colonist supports up to **5 operational facilities** ($\text{Max Operational Buildings} = \text{Living Colonists} \times 5$). Habitats are residential and exempt. Only operational and constructing facilities count; broken, buried, and deactivated buildings do not.
* **Construction**: Player-placed buildings start as `constructing`. The placement cost is paid immediately. The nearest idle colonist walks to the site and spends **6 ticks** of on-site labor before the building becomes operational. Constructing buildings produce nothing, draw nothing, and do not house colonists or spawn rovers.
* **Spacing**: A building with 0 or 1 orthogonal neighbor produces at full output. Each additional neighbor reduces production by 1 (minimum 0).
* **Aging & Lifespan**: Colonists have unique lifespans seeded between 6,000 and 10,000 ticks (6–10 Sols).
* **Automated & Direct Maintenance**: Idle colonists automatically travel to repair broken structures (when electronics are in stock) or dig out buried buildings. Repair takes **50 ticks** (30 for scrubbers) plus electronics: 1 for a habitat, solar array, scrubber, or farm, and 2 for an extractor, garage, or refinery. Digging out takes **40 ticks** and costs nothing. Operators can also dispatch maintenance or a build crew from the **Building Inspector**.

### Surface Mining & Refining Economy
* **Subterranean Ore**: 500 units of raw ore are seeded across 15–25 terrain tiles at colony generation. At least one tile holds a single unit. Deposits are fixed for the life of the colony.
* **Balanced Extraction**: **Extractors** mine only their own tile at **1 Ore per 5 ticks** for **1 PWR/tick**. An exhausted tile produces nothing, but the extractor still draws power until it is deactivated.
* **Distant Mining Sites**: Three of those deposits are placed far from the landing zone and hold about **75–95 Ore** each. A rover trip extracts **40, 50, or 60 Ore** (the site's yield × 10), limited by what remains.
* **Ore Refineries**: A player-initiated refine spends **10 Ore** to create one **Battery Cell** at 100% efficiency. A refinery stores up to **20** cells. Stored cells lose 1 efficiency per tick. Dispatch consumes one cell even at 0 efficiency.

### Autonomous Rover Logistics
* **Rover Garages**: House up to 2 autonomous exploration rovers. Rovers appear when the garage finishes construction.
* **Mining Expeditions**: Dispatch a fueled rover to one of the three distant mining sites.
* **Asteroid Interceptions**: About every **5,000 ticks** a seeded roll (50%) may spawn an asteroid. It is visible for **200 ticks** and yields **60–120 Ore**, higher than a normal mining trip. Unmined asteroids despawn.
* **Power**: A rover's own power pool is 0–150, separate from colony power. Dispatch requires one battery cell and leaves at full charge. Power drains 1.5 per tick while away and recharges 5 per tick only while idle at base.
* **Recovery Protocol**: If a rover runs out of power mid-transit, its cargo is lost and an idle colonist walks to its coordinates to tow it back to a garage with room.

### Supply Transports & Escort Protocols
* Every **300 ticks**, if habitat capacity remains (2 colonists per habitat), an interplanetary supply transport lands at Landing Pad **(0, 0)** carrying **1 new Colonist** and **2 Electronics**. No ship lands when every habitat is full.
* **150-Tick Escort Window**: A rover must be dispatched from a Garage to the landing zone before the countdown expires. On return, the arrival joins the colony and the electronics enter the stockpile. Otherwise both are lost. A colonist walking to `(0, 0)` does not count.
* **Emergency Kit**: Every colony starts with **2 Electronics**, and mechanical breakage is suppressed until **tick 500**, so a commander can build a Garage and escort the first arrival.

### Environmental Hazards & Weather
* **Dust Storms**: Beginning Sol 2.5 (tick 2,500), every 1,500-tick window has a 30% chance to bury up to 2 structures. Digging one out takes 40 ticks of colonist labor. A building that was already broken stays broken after it is dug out and still needs repair.
* **Mechanical Wear**: After tick 500, each operational or deactivated facility has a 1-in-4,000 chance per tick of breaking. Repair needs replacement electronics and engineering labor. Broken and buried buildings produce and draw nothing.

## 🖥️ UI & Control Systems

### Intelligent Mission Advisor HUD
Positioned at the top-center of the screen, the **Mission Advisor** analyzes live telemetry every tick and presents prioritized, dismissible tactical guidance:
* **Critical Alerts**: Power grid brownouts, oxygen starvation warnings, colonist health degradation.
* **Logistics Prompts**: Transport ship touchdown alerts with rover dispatch reminders.
* **Progression Advice**: Extractor site selection, battery cell refining, and agricultural expansion guidance.

### Contextual Building Inspector
Clicking any structure or the Landing Pad opens its dedicated Inspector card:
* **Status & Telemetry**: Condition (`OPERATIONAL`, `STANDBY`, `BROKEN`, `BURIED`, `CONSTRUCTING`), power draw/yield, neighbor overcrowding efficiency, and local deposit reserves.
* **Direct Facility Controls**:
  - `[ ⚡ TURN OFF POWER / RESTORE POWER ]`: Shed power load instantly. A deactivated building draws and produces nothing.
  - `[ ✥ RELOCATE (10 PWR) ]`: Move the structure to another open tile.
  - `[ 💥 DEMOLISH (10 PWR) ]`: Remove the structure. Underground ore stays on the tile.
  - `[ 🔧 DISPATCH REPAIR ]`, `[ ⛏ DISPATCH EXCAVATION ]`, and `[ 🔧 DISPATCH BUILD CREW ]`: Route the nearest idle colonist.
  - `[ 🚀 DISPATCH ROVER ESCORT ]` and `[ ⛏ DISPATCH MINING EXPEDITION ]`: Control garage rovers directly.

### Operator Settings
The settings cog (or `[S]`) pauses the simulation and holds local session controls. These are not authoritative colony state:
* **Time passes while away** (off by default). When off, closing or hiding the page freezes the clock.
* **Autopilot** (off by default, stored in this browser). When on, the client places structures, escorts arrivals, and dispatches rovers, including on a new colony.
* **Simulation speed** 1× / 2× / 3×. The HUD shows the current multiplier. `[.]` cycles it.
* **Open Mission Manual** reads building costs and survival rules from the same contract the simulation uses.

A plain-text diagnostic readout renders only when the URL includes `?debug=true`.

### Subterranean Ore Highlighting
Selecting the **Extractor tool** illuminates subterranean ore deposits, so placement can target a tile that still holds ore. Hovering a tile shows how much ore remains before an extractor is placed there.

### Interactive Landing Beacon
When an arrival touches down at `(0, 0)`, an overhead HUD beacon renders on the canvas (`[ 🚀 DISPATCH ESCORT ]`) for a one-click escort dispatch.

## 🏗️ Technical Architecture

### Deterministic Simulation Tick Engine
* **Pure Mathematical Transitions**: Given state $S_t$ and seed $\sigma$, $S_{t+1} = \text{tick}(S_t, \sigma)$. Applying $N$ ticks in a single batch produces the exact same outcome as applying $N$ individual ticks.
* **Seeded PRNG**: Breakage, storm timing, asteroid timing and position, ore placement, lifespans, and movement tie-breaks use the colony's seeded generator. The simulation does not call `Math.random()` for those decisions.
* **Authoritative writes**: The client does not run the tick on a timer. Actions and ticks go through the Supabase `action` and `tick` Edge Functions. If those functions are unreachable, `server-simulation.ts` applies the same rules directly. Client projection between writes never rewinds the tick counter.

### Isometric Mathematics & Render Pipeline
* **Coordinate Transforms**: A 2:1 isometric projection maps screen $(x, y) \leftrightarrow \text{grid}(u, v)$ on a 20×20 grid. Tile `(0, 0)` is the landing pad and cannot be built on.
* **Depth and Picking**: Buildings are created in back-to-front grid order; the WebGL depth buffer resolves mesh occlusion. Raycasting intersects elevated terrain and building meshes for placement and inspection.

### Authoritative Server & Database Design
* **Owned tables**: `marscolony_users`, `marscolony_colonies`, `marscolony_buildings`, `marscolony_colonists`, `marscolony_rovers`, and `marscolony_ore_deposits`. Row-Level Security scopes each table to the signed-in user.
* **JSONB on the colony row**: Battery cells, mining sites, the active asteroid, and pending arrivals. `bestSolsSurvived` lives on `marscolony_users` and is updated only by the server tick at game over.
* This app does not read or write `waypoint_` tables. See [DATABASE.md](DATABASE.md).

Rules and numbers live in [CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md). Visual rules live in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

## 🛠️ Getting Started

### Prerequisites
* **Node.js**: `v18.0.0` or higher (production on Vercel uses Node 24)
* **npm**: `v9.0.0` or higher
* **Supabase CLI** (optional, for deploying Edge Functions): `v2.0.0+`

### Installation & Setup

1. **Clone the repository**:
   ```bash
   git clone https://github.com/JacintoDesign/ztm-mars-colony-astra.git
   cd ztm-mars-colony-astra
   ```

2. **Install dependencies**:
   ```bash
   npm install
   ```

3. **Configure environment variables**:
   Copy `.env.example` to `.env`. The browser client accepts either prefix:
   ```env
   VITE_SUPABASE_URL=https://your-project.supabase.co
   VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY=your-anon-key
   ```
   `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` work as aliases. The service role key is not read by the Vite app. Do not put it in a `VITE_` or `NEXT_PUBLIC_` variable.

4. **Start the local development server**:
   ```bash
   npm run dev
   ```
   Open [http://localhost:5173](http://localhost:5173) to play, or [http://localhost:5173/model-preview.html](http://localhost:5173/model-preview.html) for the model showcase.

5. **Build for production**:
   ```bash
   npm run build
   ```
   Production deploys from the `main` branch of this repo to [ztm-mars-colony.vercel.app](https://ztm-mars-colony.vercel.app).

### Edge Function Deployment

To deploy the simulation functions to Supabase:
```bash
npx supabase functions deploy tick
npx supabase functions deploy action
```

### 3D renderer verification

With the dev server running, open `/tests/renderer-check.html`. This isolated browser check verifies terrain picking at all 400 tile centers, picking all seven buildings, rendering operational, broken, buried, and deactivated states, and that rendering does not mutate the source fixture. It requires no Supabase account and performs no network writes.
